Trade Craft — reading guide

How to search a stock and actually understand what you're looking at

Type a company name into Trade Craft and you get a chart, a handful of number tiles, and an AI-written paragraph. This is what each part is telling you, and how to read them together rather than one at a time.

1. Searching

Getting to the right listing, not just a listing.

1
Type a name or a ticker
Either works — nvidia and NVDA find the same company. Names work on any exchange in the world, not just the US.
2
Pick the right listing if you're asked
Some companies trade in more than one place — a home listing plus a thin U.S. ADR, say. When it's genuinely ambiguous, you'll see a picker with a badge (OTC, Secondary) on the thinner lines, so you don't land on the wrong one by accident.
3
The chart and metrics load first — for free
Price, indicators, patterns, news, and company info need no key or account. The AI paragraph (below) is the only piece that needs your own free Anthropic key.

2. The chart itself

Everything else on the page is explaining what this is showing.

→
Candles vs. Line
A candle shows open/high/low/close for that bar — green means the price closed above where it opened, red means below. Line just tracks the close, useful for seeing the overall shape without the noise.
→
Timeframes (1Y · 1M · 10D · 5D · 1D)
Same stock, different zoom. A stock can look calm on 1Y and volatile on 1D at the same time — neither view is "more correct," they answer different questions (the trend vs. what's happening right now).
→
Patterns
Tap Patterns to have the app scan the visible chart for a recognizable shape and draw it directly on the candles, with a plain-English explanation underneath.

3. The metric tiles

Tap any tile in the app to get this same explanation written for that stock's own numbers.

RSI (14) 0–100
Compresses recent up-moves vs. down-moves into one number.
Below 30 = oversold, selling may be overdone. Above 70 = overbought, buying may be overdone. 30–70 = neutral. A momentum gauge, not a signal on its own.
MACD (12,26,9)
The gap between a fast and slow moving average, smoothed by a signal line.
MACD above signal, histogram growing = momentum building up. Below signal, growing negative = momentum fading or turning down.
SMA 50 / SMA 200
The average closing price over the last 50 (medium-term) or 200 (long-term) sessions.
Price sitting above both lines generally reads as an intact uptrend; below both as a downtrend. SMA50 crossing above SMA200 is the classic "golden cross."
Bollinger Bands (20,2)
A 20-day average with a band drawn two standard deviations above and below it.
Price near the upper band = strength, or short-term overextension. Bands widening = rising volatility; squeezing together = calm, often before a move.
Volume vs. 20d
Today's shares traded, compared to the 20-day average, as a percentage.
This is the confirmation layer. A big price move on volume far above average is trustworthy; the same move on thin volume is weaker — fewer people actually voted with money.
Beta
How much a stock swings for a given move in its benchmark index.
1.0 = moves in step with the market. Above 1 = amplified swings (more volatile). Below 1 = muted swings.

4. The AI momentum read

Everything above, synthesized into a paragraph — and shown its work.

bullish · confidence: moderate

Price sits above both moving averages with a positive MACD histogram — an intact uptrend. RSI is neutral, so momentum isn't stretched either way, and today's volume is below average, which tempers confidence in the move.

Fact Price ($213.90) is above SMA50 ($213.67) and SMA200 ($197.81).
Interpretation Being above both averages is generally read as the medium- and long-term trend staying upward.

Every claim is labeled Fact (a number straight from the data) or Interpretation (what that number is commonly read to mean) — so you can always tell where the hard data stops and the reading begins. It never says buy or sell.

5. Fundamentals & ecosystem

The slower-moving picture — who the company is, not just what the chart did today.

P/E ratioShare price ÷ earnings per share — roughly, how much investors are paying for each dollar the company earns.
EPSNet profit ÷ shares outstanding — the company's earnings attributable to one share.
Revenue growth% change in total sales vs. the prior comparable period.
Profit marginNet profit as a % of revenue — how much of every sales dollar the company actually keeps.
Debt / EquityTotal debt relative to shareholder equity — how much the company leans on borrowing.
PeersOther companies shown alongside this one — same-industry competitors where available, or ones investors commonly view together.
One thing to keep in mind: nothing on this page is a signal, and nothing tells you to buy or sell. It describes what the numbers are doing and how that's typically read — the interpretation, and the decision, are always yours.
